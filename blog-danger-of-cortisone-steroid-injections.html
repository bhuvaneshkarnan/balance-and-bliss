The Hidden Danger of Cortisone Injections for Heel Spurs: Why Tendon Rupture Risks Are Real

When morning heel pain becomes agonizing, orthopedic clinics frequently offer a 'quick fix': a corticosteroid injection directly into the heel. Discover why this temporary relief carries dangerous, irreversible risks for your foot architecture.

Steroid injections into the heel risk permanent plantar fat pad atrophy and fascial rupture.

The Catabolic Nature of Corticosteroids on Collagen

Cortisone is a powerful catabolic hormone. While it violently suppresses local inflammation and eliminates pain for 4 to 8 weeks, it simultaneously inhibits fibroblast proliferation, halts healthy collagen synthesis, and degrades the extracellular matrix of the plantar fascia.

The Devastating Complication: Plantar Fat Pad Atrophy

Repeated cortisone injections cause the natural, shock-absorbing calcaneal fat pad to permanently shrink and die. Once this fat cushion is gone, the bare calcaneus strikes the floor directly with every step, creating permanent, untreatable heel pain.

Doctor's Warning: Do Not Sacrifice Your Fat Pad

Cortisone merely numbs the alarm without putting out the fire. Preserve your natural collagen and heal your heel through mechanical, drugless reflexology.
