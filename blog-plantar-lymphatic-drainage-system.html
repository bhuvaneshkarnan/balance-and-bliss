How Interstitial Fluid Becomes Trapped in the Extremities

Every day, approximately 2 to 3 liters of interstitial fluid, cellular debris, and metabolic proteins are filtered into the lymphatic capillaries. When sedentary lifestyles or gravitational stasis stall this flow, fluid stagnates in the dependent lower extremities, causing puffy ankles and tissue heaviness.

Stimulating the Dorsal and Plantar Lymphatic Zones

Dr. Mehraj utilizes feather-light, rhythmic pumping strokes along the interdigital web spaces and the dorsal lymphatic arch. This gentle stretch stimulates the intrinsic contractility of lymphatic angions, opening endothelial flap valves and siphoning fluid upward into the inguinal nodes.

Clinical Key: Lymph Requires Gentle Rhythms

Lymphatic capillaries are fragile and collapse under heavy force. Very light, rhythmic reflex stimulation is the golden key to draining trapped fluid.
