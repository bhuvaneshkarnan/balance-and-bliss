Morton's Neuroma Relief: Eliminating Burning Forefoot Nerve Pain Without Surgery

Feeling like you are constantly walking on a folded sock or a sharp pebble under the ball of your foot? Morton's neuroma is a painful swelling of the digital nerve between your toes that can be resolved without surgical nerve excision.

Dr. M. Mehraj Abdullah, M.D (Acu) ATAMA Reg. No: 20261714 / TN • October 19, 2026 • 5 min read
Forefoot intermetatarsal decompression releases pinched plantar digital nerves.

How Narrow Footwear and Metatarsal Collapse Pinch the Digital Nerve

Morton's neuroma is not a true tumor, but a perineural thickening of the common plantar digital nerve, most commonly between the 3rd and 4th metatarsal heads. Narrow shoes squeeze the metatarsals together, compressing the nerve against the deep transverse metatarsal ligament and causing electrical sensations, numbness, and burning.

Restoring the Transverse Metatarsal Arch via Reflex Point Splay

Dr. Mehraj utilizes manual metatarsal splaying techniques combined with precision reflex stimulation on the dorsal and plantar nerve junctions. By expanding the transverse metatarsal arch, natural anatomical space is restored around the nerve, halting the chronic compression loop immediately.

Clinical Recommendation: Stop Squeezing Your Toes

Wearing wide toe-box footwear and receiving transverse metatarsal reflex decompression creates the physical space needed for the swollen nerve to heal naturally.

Avoiding Surgical Neurectomy and Permanent Forefoot Numbness

Surgical excision of the neuroma permanently cuts the sensory nerve, leaving patients with numbness in their toes and the risk of agonizing 'stump neuromas'. Non-invasive clinical reflexology preserves nerve function while eliminating the compressive cause.
